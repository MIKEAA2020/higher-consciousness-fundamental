1. Executive Summary
This fifth edition exists because three new classes of material entered the corpus, and each changes what
the audit must do. The first class is an external adversarial audit: a ninety-line, line-referenced critique of
the 130-turn dialogue, authored by a third-party model (Claude) and supplied by the interlocutor with the
instruction that it be read line by line and its opposing points adjudicated rather than absorbed or
dismissed. It is the eleventh party to audit the framework. The second class is a pair of exported critique
files — the documents named “Not conclusively. It is a recognisable.txt” and “It is substantially stronger,
but it still.txt” — which had been attached, unread as sources, inside two DeepSeek exchanges since the
rounds in which those exchanges were recovered. Their verbatim contents are now read from the file
service and recorded: they are the two sequential critiques of the contingency argument for a necessary
ground, and together they constitute the twelfth auditing party. The third class is empirical: two
dissociative-identity-disorder papers supplied as PDFs and read in full (Schlumpf et al. 2013; Reinders et
al. 2019), joining the three sources read in the prior round (Santander et al. 2025; Modesti et al. 2022; the
Reynante re-analysis toolkit over the Farnes et al. 2020 data) to give every one of the decombination
program’s four domains at least one verified empirical anchor.
The governing verdict of the Claude audit’s adjudication is that it is largely sustained, and that this is good
news for the framework rather than bad. Its “what survives” table concedes the core: consciousness-first
monism as defensible metaphysics, the intrinsic/extrinsic split as the cleanest bridge to physics,
objectivity as invariance rather than privileged frame, and non-composite ground. Its attacks land almost
entirely on the transcript’s claim discipline — proof language for non-proofs, equivalence counted as
validation, opposite cosmological findings both claimed as confirmation, teleological narratives promoted
as rigorous proof, pasted outside-model text answered as the interlocutor’s own — and on naming and
scoping failures that the consolidated editions had already begun to repair. Where the audit is wrong, it is
wrong in two documentable ways: the pilot-wave revaluation it calls a flip with no new evidence is
announced and motivated in the transcript itself (the arc-test retraction covers exactly this case), and its
charge that nothing enforces the two-language discipline is true of the transcript but false of the
consolidations, which enforce it with an atemporal lexicon and a graded-claims regime. One reciprocity
finding cuts against the auditor: two of the unverifiable physics items it flags could not be located
anywhere in the transcript by this edition’s own verification pass, so the rule it establishes — unverified
citations are excluded from the empirical ledger — is applied to the auditor’s own examples.
The empirical anchors change the status of the decombination program without changing its evidential
weight against physicalism, and the edition is explicit that these are different things. The Santander
split-brain study anchors the partition threshold structure: a patient retaining roughly one centimetre of
the splenium — about ten percent of the corpus callosum — with full interhemispheric integration and no
disconnection syndrome, a result the authors themselves describe as “a unique type of criticality.” The
Modesti review anchors the switching structure: across thirteen studies, identity-state switches in DID
converge on a caudate signature, a subcortical partition-switching mechanism. The Farnes and Reynante
ketamine materials anchor the complexity instrument: a complete re-analysis pipeline — Hilbert

Why the filenames are

DeepSeek’s file-export naming convention: an exported response is named by the truncated
opening words of the response it contains — approximately the first forty characters of its first
sentence. The names are sentence prefixes, not titles, so they read as broken clauses. The
convention is platform behavior, not corruption; the file contents are intact and match the
responses the model gave when the files were re-attached.

their contents as sources: the corpus carried only DeepSeek’s response-mediated concessions.

and this edition closes that gap by recording them as the twelfth party and adjudicating their
contents directly (chapter 7).

2.3 The status-tag regime and the provenance register

3. The Arc of the Corpus, Extended
The corpus arc now terminates in a four-way structure that no prior edition had: a dialogue line that
produced the framework, an external-exchange line that formalized and critiqued it, an audit lineage that
consolidated it, and — new in this round — an adversarial external audit plus an empirical source set. The
Claude audit sits outside both dialogue ecosystems, which gives it a function no prior party had: it tests
whether the framework’s claim discipline survives a reader with no stake in either line’s dialectical
history. Its verdict — core defensible, superstructure relabeling, several “proofs” in contradiction with the
premise — is what an unsympathetic strong reader concludes from the transcript alone. That is exactly
the reader a steelman must satisfy, which is why the adjudication in chapter 6 treats the audit’s sustained
findings as repair instructions rather than as attacks.
The two exported critiques sit at a different position: inside the DeepSeek line’s contingency thread. That
thread ran from the framework’s theological culmination (the necessary, purely actual, simple, unique
ground) back down to its argumentative foundations, and the two files are its strongest moments — the
points where the argument’s own auditor refused to let the conclusion outrun its premises. The
consolidated editions had adopted the thread’s outcome (the conditional core) without possessing the
thread’s instrument (the verbatim critique apparatus). Possessing it changes the audit’s obligation: the
necessary-ground thesis is now graded against the eight-step defensible core the second file states, and the
grading is stricter than any prior edition applied.
The empirical source set sits at the corpus’s newest position: outside all dialogue lines entirely, in the

4. The Three Empirical Anchors
An anchor, as this edition uses the term, is a verified empirical finding in the open literature that satisfies
three conditions: it is read from the source rather than from secondary discussion; it maps onto a specific
structure of the decombination specification (the partition threshold, the switching mechanism, or the
complexity measure); and its data pathway is identified — where the raw material lives and under what
conditions it can be re-analyzed. An anchor is not a discriminating prediction. Nothing in this chapter
separates consciousness-first monism from physicalism, and the edition adopts as a governing rule the
audit’s sharpest methodological point: empirical equivalence is neutral for the framework, not
supportive — the rule this chapter labels equivalence-is-not-validation. What the anchors do is different:

4.1 Split-brain: the Santander criticality result
Santander et al. (2025, PNAS 122(43):e2520190122) studied six adult callosotomy patients with
neuroimaging and behavioral interhemispheric-transfer testing. Two patients had partial sections; the
critical case is patient BT, who retained approximately one centimetre of the splenium — roughly ten
percent of the corpus callosum’s cross-sectional area, about ninety percent lost — yet showed full
interhemispheric integration, including tactile information transfer, with no disconnection syndrome. The
paper’s own language is the anchor: a “unique type of criticality” in which “a small proportion of posterior
callosal fibers may be sufficient” for integrated function. The analysis code is open (the
tsantander/splitBrainNetworks repository); patient data are available on request only, consistent with the
prior round’s finding that split-brain raw data has no fully open corpus.
What this anchors in the program is the threshold structure. The decombination specification’s corrected
core holds that partitions form when the coupling parameter falls below a critical value — domains form
when coupling drops below the critical fraction — with two thresholds rather than one, and the
specification’s own honesty clause marks the four-domain predictions conjectural until the neural
parameter map exists. The Santander result is the first empirical cousin of that structure: integration
survives radical substrate reduction, and the survival has a threshold character rather than a linear one.
The program’s split-brain track now has a finding to model (what is the posterior-fiber fraction below
which integration collapses, and is the collapse the spec’s predicted second threshold?), an instrument to
model it with (the open analysis pipeline), and a failure condition (if integration degrades linearly with
fiber fraction across the full patient series, the critical-threshold reading of this case is an artifact of one
patient, and the spec’s threshold claim loses its only direct anchor). The finding remains, in the status
vocabulary, Empirical-Verified as a result and Interpretive Mapping as a confirmation of the program —
the mapping is ours, not the paper’s.
4.2 DID: the Modesti caudate-switching result
Modesti et al. (2022, Journal of Personalized Medicine 12:1405 — the paper the prior round could only
identify as bot-walled PMC9502311) is a systematic review of functional neuroimaging in dissociative
disorders: thirteen studies published 2006 through 2022, fifty-one DID patients in total. Its convergent
finding is the anchor: identity-state alterations in DID are consistently associated with alterations of the
caudate nucleus and its functional network, with the review describing a “shift from hippocampal
involvement to caudate involvement” across identity states, alongside prefrontal and anterior-cingulate
findings. The paper is fully open (the interlocutor supplied the PDF; it was read to its references in the

4.3 Ketamine: the Farnes data and the Reynante pipeline

What this anchors in the program is the complexity measure itself. The decombination specification’s

5. The DID Domain, Deepened
5.1 Schlumpf et al. 2013: part-dependent preconscious reactivity
Schlumpf et al. (2013, NeuroImage: Clinical 3:54–64) is the first fMRI study of preconscious emotional
processing in dissociative identity disorder, and it supplied this round’s most directly relevant PDF (read
in full, all eleven pages, body to references). Fifteen DID patients and fifteen matched healthy actors
were exposed, as both a neutral identity part (ANP) and an emotional part (EP), to angry and neutral
faces masked at 16.7 milliseconds — below the threshold of conscious perception, verified by subjective

and forced-choice awareness tests. The final neural analysis covered eleven patients and fifteen controls;
the behavioral analysis thirteen patients. The design’s core comparison is between-part and
between-group: DID-EP versus DID-ANP, and genuine parts versus instructed simulation.
Three findings matter for the program. First, part-dependent preconscious signatures: as EP, patients
showed elevated activation in the right anterior parahippocampal gyrus relative to their own ANP in both
face conditions; against actors simulating EP, genuine EP showed — for neutral faces — a large,
family-wise-error corrected cluster (peak in the dorsal brainstem, 1,729 voxels) spanning the
occipito-temporal junction (lingual, fusiform), parahippocampal cortex, and motor-related areas
including pre-supplementary motor area and precentral gyrus. As ANP, patients showed reduced
activation across comparisons — the paper’s own reading is a globally subdued preconscious engagement.
Second, the neutral-face inversion: the differentiating stimulus was the emotionally ambiguous neutral
face, not the angry one; EP’s reaction times to neutral faces were the longest in the design (a large effect
against ANP), which the authors read as threat-ambiguity hypervigilance in the part fixed in traumatic
memory. Third, the simulation control failed: the actors, instructed and motivated to enact ANP and
EP after watching a therapy video and practicing, tended to invert the patterns — reacting as ANP the
way genuine EP did and conversely — and could not reproduce the genuine parts’ psychobiological
signatures at the preconscious level.
For the decombination program this is the strongest single anchor in the corpus. The spec’s first
criterion — distinct partitions with distinct processing signatures — is here demonstrated within-subject,
preconsciously, against an active simulation control, in the domain the program itself named. The
finding’s status is Empirical-Verified as a result; the program’s reading of it (that the signatures are
partition structure rather than, say, trauma-driven attentional bias) remains an Interpretive Mapping, and
the paper’s own limitations are carried with it: small samples, mostly medicated patients, blockwise
design that may mask amygdala effects, and a treated population in which natural differences are
plausibly underestimated.
5.2 Reinders et al. 2019: individual-level neuroanatomical
classification
Reinders et al. (2019, British Journal of Psychiatry 215:536–544; doi 10.1192/bjp.2018.255) is the
pattern-recognition study behind the news item the prior round pinned to King’s College London — the
second PDF read in full this round (all nine pages). Seventy-five women across three scanning centers:
thirty-two with DID (diagnosed by SCID-D expert interview, specifically to exclude imitative DID) and
forty-three matched healthy controls. Gaussian-process classifiers were trained on deformation-based
morphometry — scalar-momentum features from diffeomorphic registration of grey and white matter —
with leave-one-participant-out cross-validation and feature selection inside each training fold. The
classifiers discriminated DID from controls at the individual level with 71.9 percent sensitivity and 73.8
percent specificity: balanced accuracy 72.8 percent, area under the curve 0.74, significantly above chance
by permutation test.

5.3 The assembled domain: four anchors, one structure
The DID domain now carries four verified anchors that compose into the program’s partition
architecture: state-dependent preconscious processing with a failed simulation control (Schlumpf 2013); a
switching signature in the caudate network (Modesti 2022, review level); an individual-level structural

reported statistics and any data the groups share on request. The honest composite status:
Empirical-Verified findings, an assembled structure that matches the spec’s first three criteria, an
Interpretive-Mapping reading of that match, and no discrimination against physicalism — a physicalist
neuroscience of trauma-induced network reorganization predicts compatible facts, and the consolidation
does not pretend otherwise.

7.2 The first critique: “Not conclusively”
The first file’s seven vulnerabilities decompose into three that the consolidated editions had already
absorbed and four that sharpen the grading. Already absorbed: the strong Principle of Sufficient Reason
is a substantive premise, not a theorem (the corpus’s A-tier now carries it as a stated premise with its
rejection priced); the rejection of brute fact and infinite regress requires argument, not dissatisfaction
(the rivals table prices both as standing alternatives); and the conclusion’s property list outruns its
premises (the conditional core). Sharpening: the dichotomy of premise two is false — an external ground

termination; “self-existent” does not immediately mean essence-entails-existence without further modal
premises; the move from physics’ failure to entail existence to physical reality’s contingency is a category
shift (a theory’s not entailing its subject’s necessity is an epistemic fact about theories); and the category
correction — physicalism is a doctrine, not an entity, so the conclusion must read “fundamental physical
reality cannot be the ultimate self-existent ground” — is adopted verbatim into the axiom statement. The
file’s own stronger formulation, six steps ending at a ground that is not physical “in the same dependent

8.2 The anesthesia fork, stated
The interlocutor’s formalization turn predicted that under anesthesia the idealist reading is “loss of the
boundary, so experience ceases even if some local processing continues” (L4006); the assistant’s reply
held that “the boundary becomes too rigid or fragmented, so the alter’s access to the environment is
severed (loss of reportability), but the fundamental field remains” (L4081). These are different empirical
claims: cessation of experience versus severance of access with persistence of the field. The fork is
genuine, and the consolidation’s duty is to state it rather than harmonize it. Under the status regime both
readings are Speculation; what distinguishes them is their priced relationship to evidence. The cessation
reading is aligned with the floor protocol’s anesthesia test (graded sedation depth with reports at recovery)
and is the weaker metaphysical commitment; the access-severance reading preserves the field’s continuity
but takes on the burden of saying what an unreported, access-severed experience is, which is the
locus-of-ignorance problem in empirical dress. The program’s anesthesia track — now anchored by the
open sedation corpora and the shared complexity instrument — can in principle adjudicate between
them: graded-depth designs with pre-registered recovery reports distinguish cessation from access loss
only at the phenomenological margin, so the fork is written into the track’s failure conditions (Table 4.1):
if the graded-depth design cannot separate the readings, the access-severance reading is unfalsifiable in
this domain and must be re-graded from Speculation to an explicitly metaphysical commitment.

13. The Empirical Annex: The Anchored
Program
The annex of the prior editions carried two programs: the wager on a rival’s physics (the Valentini
non-equilibrium probe) and the decombination program built from ontologically neutral mathematics.
Both carry forward with their statuses unchanged — the first Empirical-Unverified by its own nature, the
second now anchored per Table 4.1. What the annex gains in this edition is the governing discipline stated
as doctrine, and the run order. The discipline: the annex reports runs, not rhetoric; every run is
pre-registered against a stated failure condition; equivalence results are reported as neutral; and no result
is mapped onto the framework without carrying the Interpretive-Mapping tag. The run order follows
instrument readiness: first the ketamine pipeline (the instrument is complete and the data open); then the
anesthesia corpora (the instrument family is shared); then the split-brain analysis code over the
request-only patient data; then the DID track through the reported-statistics route, since raw data remains
closed. The DID domain’s assembled anchors make it the program’s strongest explanatory target even as
its data pathway is the weakest — a tension the annex states rather than resolves.
The annex also records what the anchors cannot be made to do, because the transcript’s history shows the
temptation is systematic. The Santander criticality result cannot be announced as confirmation of the
threshold structure until the threshold reading survives the full patient series. The caudate-switching
finding cannot be announced as the switching mechanism until individual-level dynamics discriminate the
coupled-bistable, winner-take-all, and graded-mixture readings. The complexity pipeline cannot be
announced as validating the partition criterion until it runs without the artifact confounds it is designed to
expose. Each of these would be exactly the retrofit pattern the Claude audit documented — result first,
mapping after — and each is therefore written here as a barred move before any result exists. The annex’s
first pre-registered run, whenever it occurs, is the program’s first opportunity to fail honestly, which is the
only credential a research program cannot grant itself.
