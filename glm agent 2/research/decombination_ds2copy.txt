# The Decombination Problem: Rigorous Formulation

---

## 1. Primitives

**Base space.** Let \( \mathcal{B} \) be a connected, locally finite graph \( G = (V, E) \) with vertices \( V \) and edges \( E \). \( \mathcal{B} \) is the substrate of experience. (A manifold with a finite cover is an alternative; the graph case is chosen for concreteness and computability.)

**Qualia space.** Let \( \mathcal{Q} \) be a complete, separable metric space with a convex structure—e.g., a convex subset of a Riemannian manifold. \( \mathcal{Q} \) carries the topology of experiential similarity.

**Universal field.** A **configuration** is a section
\[
\Psi: V \to \mathcal{Q}, \quad \Psi(i) = \psi_i.
\]
The space of configurations is \( \Omega = \mathcal{Q}^V \).

**Alter.** An **alter** is a connected subset \( A \subseteq V \) with boundary
\[
\partial A = \{ i \in A : \exists j \notin A, (i,j) \in E \}.
\]
The experience of \( A \) is \( \Psi|_A \).

**Partition.** A **partition** of \( \mathcal{B} \) is a collection \( \mathcal{P} = \{A_1, \dots, A_N\} \) of connected subsets with \( A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset \) and \( \bigcup_i A_i = V \).

---

## 2. Atemporal Variational Principle

Time is extrinsic. The fundamental object is a probability measure on \( \Omega \), not a temporal flow. Define the **free energy functional**
\[
F_\beta[\Psi] = E[\Psi] + \frac{1}{\beta} S[\Psi],
\]
where:
- \( E[\Psi] \) is an **energy** penalizing inhomogeneity across edges,
- \( S[\Psi] \) is an **entropy** or complexity term,
- \( \beta > 0 \) is an inverse integration parameter.

The **equilibrium measure** is
\[
p_\beta[\Psi] = \frac{1}{Z_\beta} \exp\big(-\beta F_\beta[\Psi]\big), \quad
Z_\beta = \int_\Omega \exp\big(-\beta F_\beta[\Psi]\big) \, d\Psi.
\]

No time parameter appears. Temporal evolution is the extrinsic image of sampling from \( p_\beta \).

**Energy.** For \( \Psi \in \Omega \),
\[
E[\Psi] = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} J_{ij} \, d(\psi_i, \psi_j)^2,
\]
where \( J_{ij} \geq 0 \) are coupling strengths and \( d \) is the metric on \( \mathcal{Q} \).

**Complexity.** \( S[\Psi] \) is chosen so that maintaining all couplings is costly:
\[
S[\Psi] = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} C(J_{ij}), \quad C(J) = J \log J - J.
\]
This is a standard entropy penalty; alternatives are possible.

---

## 3. Subject Conditions

An alter \( A \subseteq V \) is a **subject** iff it satisfies:

**S1 (Integration).** Define the **intrinsic integration measure**
\[
\Phi(\Psi|_A) = \min_{\substack{A_1 \cup A_2 = A \\ A_1 \cap A_2 = \emptyset}} \; I\big(\Psi|_{A_1} ; \Psi|_{A_2} \,\big|\, \Psi|_{\partial A_1 \cap \partial A_2}\big),
\]
where \( I \) is mutual information under \( p_\beta \). Require
\[
\Phi(\Psi|_A) > \theta
\]
for a threshold \( \theta > 0 \). This replaces the physicalist \( \Phi \) of IIT with an intrinsic measure on the experiential field.

**S2 (Dissociation).** The boundary screens off \( A \) from its complement:
\[
I\big(\Psi|_A ; \Psi|_{V \setminus A} \,\big|\, \Psi|_{\partial A}\big) = 0.
\]
The boundary \( \partial A \) is part of the alter's experience; it is the interface with the "external" field.

**S3 (Self-model).** There exists \( A_{\text{self}} \subseteq A \) such that \( \Psi|_{A_{\text{self}}} \) encodes a generative model of \( \partial A \) and \( A \). Formally, there is a map
\[
g: \mathcal{Q}^{A_{\text{self}}} \to \mathcal{Q}^{\partial A} \times \mathcal{Q}^A
\]
that is surjective onto the relevant support of \( p_\beta \).

**S4 (Perspective).** The self-model has a unique fixed point: there exists \( \psi^* \in \mathcal{Q}^{A_{\text{self}}} \) such that
\[
g(\psi^*) = (\psi^*|_{\partial A}, \psi^*|_A)
\]
and \( \psi^* \) is the unique such point in the support of \( p_\beta \). This formalizes "single first-person perspective."

---

## 4. The Decombination Problem

**Given:** A configuration \( \Psi \) on \( \mathcal{B} \) such that no proper subset \( A \subsetneq V \) satisfies S1–S4. That is, \( \mathcal{B} \) is one subject with no internal boundaries.

**Find:** An energy functional \( E \) and parameter \( \beta \) such that the equilibrium measure \( p_\beta \) concentrates on configurations \( \Psi \) for which there exists a partition \( \mathcal{P} = \{A_1, \dots, A_N\} \), \( N > 1 \), with each \( A_i \) satisfying S1–S4.

**Mathematical constraints:**
1. **Intrinsic:** No external designer. \( E \) is defined solely in terms of \( \Psi \) and \( \mathcal{B} \).
2. **Local:** \( E \) depends only on edges of \( \mathcal{B} \).
3. **Variational:** \( E \) is derived from a principle, not stipulated.
4. **Existence:** There exists \( \beta_c \) such that for \( \beta > \beta_c \), the homogeneous configuration is not the unique minimizer of \( F_\beta \).

**Empirical constraints (separate):**
5. For parameters corresponding to the human brain, the resulting partition matches known neural and phenomenological data.

---

## 5. Order Parameter and Bifurcation

Define the **modularity**
\[
M(\Psi) = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} \big(J_{ij} - \langle J \rangle\big) \, \delta(\psi_i, \psi_j),
\]
where \( \delta(\psi_i, \psi_j) = 1 \) if \( \psi_i \) and \( \psi_j \) are in the same alter and \( 0 \) otherwise. \( M \) measures the degree of partitioning.

The **expected modularity** is
\[
\langle M \rangle_\beta = \int_\Omega M(\Psi) \, p_\beta[\Psi] \, d\Psi.
\]

**Bifurcation hypothesis:** There exists \( \beta_c \) such that:
- For \( \beta < \beta_c \): \( \langle M \rangle_\beta = 0 \) (one subject).
- For \( \beta > \beta_c \): \( \langle M \rangle_\beta > 0 \) (multiple subjects).

This is a symmetry-breaking phase transition. The decombination law is the statement of this bifurcation.

---

## 6. Toy Model

Let \( \mathcal{Q} = \mathbb{R} \), \( \mathcal{B} \) a ring of \( N \) nodes with nearest-neighbor edges, \( J_{ij} = J \) for all edges. Energy:
\[
E[\Psi] = J \sum_{(i,j)} (\psi_i - \psi_j)^2 + \lambda \sum_i \psi_i^2.
\]
This is a Gaussian model. The partition function is computable:
\[
Z_\beta = \int \exp\left(-\beta J \sum_{(i,j)} (\psi_i - \psi_j)^2 - \beta \lambda \sum_i \psi_i^2\right) d\Psi.
\]
The covariance matrix is \( C = (\beta J L + \beta \lambda I)^{-1} \), where \( L \) is the graph Laplacian.

**Modularity** is determined by the correlation structure. For \( \lambda \) large, all nodes are independent: \( M = 0 \). For \( \lambda \) small, correlations span the whole ring: one subject. At intermediate \( \lambda \), the ring breaks into clusters. The critical \( \lambda_c \) is determined by the spectral gap of \( L \).

This model is exactly solvable. It demonstrates the bifurcation but is too simple to produce human-like alters. It is a proof of concept.

---

## 7. Neural Mapping Functor

Let \( \mathbf{Exp} \) be the category whose objects are pairs \( (A, \Psi|_A) \) with \( A \subseteq V \) and morphisms are inclusions preserving boundaries. Let \( \mathbf{Neu} \) be the category whose objects are pairs \( (M, \partial M) \) where \( M \) is a neural system and \( \partial M \) is a Markov blanket, and morphisms are inclusions preserving blankets.

Define a functor
\[
R: \mathbf{Exp} \to \mathbf{Neu}
\]
that maps \( (A, \Psi|_A) \) to \( (M_A, \partial M_A) \), where \( M_A \) is the neural system whose dynamics are the extrinsic image of \( \Psi|_A \), and \( \partial M_A \) is the neural Markov blanket corresponding to \( \partial A \). \( R \) must preserve conditional independence:
\[
I(\Psi|_A ; \Psi|_{V \setminus A} \mid \Psi|_{\partial A}) = 0
\iff
I(x_{M_A} ; x_{\text{ext}} \mid x_{\partial M_A}) = 0.
\]

This makes the mapping from experiential boundaries to neural Markov blankets a formal correspondence, not an analogy.

---

## 8. Fate of the Original Subject

Decombination is **differentiation**, not fission. The universal field \( \Psi \) on \( \mathcal{B} \) remains the whole. The alters are restrictions \( \Psi|_{A_i} \). The universal field does not cease to be a subject in its own right; it is the ground. But for the problem's purposes, the homogeneous configuration is unstable, and the stable configurations are the partitioned ones. The universal field is not itself an alter unless it satisfies S1–S4, which it does not when partitioned.

---

## 9. Mathematical vs Empirical Problems

**Mathematical problem:** Existence, stability, uniqueness, and bifurcation structure of the partition. This is a problem in statistical mechanics and dynamical systems.

**Empirical problem:** Mapping \( \partial A_i \) to neural Markov blankets and testing predictions. This is a problem in neuroscience.

The two are linked by the functor \( R \), but they are distinct. The mathematical problem can be solved independently; the empirical problem constrains the choice of \( E \).

---

## 10. Summary

The decombination problem is now a self-contained, rigorous problem in statistical mechanics:

> **Find an energy functional \( E \) on a graph \( \mathcal{B} \) with qualia space \( \mathcal{Q} \) such that the equilibrium measure \( p_\beta \propto \exp(-\beta F) \) undergoes a symmetry-breaking phase transition at \( \beta_c \), yielding a partition into subsets satisfying S1–S4.**

The problem is atemporal, intrinsic, local, and variational. It has a toy model, an order parameter, and a formal mapping to neuroscience. It is feasible with current mathematical tools. The remaining work is to solve the bifurcation problem for realistic \( E \) and to confirm the neural mapping empirically.















# Solution to the Decombination Problem

## 1. The Model

Let \( G = (V, E) \) be a connected graph with \( |V| = N \). Let \( L \) be the graph Laplacian:
\[
L_{ij} = \begin{cases} \deg(i) & i = j \\ -1 & (i,j) \in E \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\]

The **experiential field** is \( \Psi: V \to \mathbb{R} \), with the constraint
\[
\sum_{i \in V} \psi_i = 0.
\]

The **energy functional** is
\[
E[\Psi] = \Psi^T L \Psi - \mu \|\Psi\|^2,
\]
where \( \mu > 0 \) is the integration parameter.

The **equilibrium measure** at inverse temperature \( \beta \) is
\[
p_\beta[\Psi] \propto \exp\left(-\beta E[\Psi]\right).
\]

---

## 2. The Ground State

The quadratic form \( \Psi^T L \Psi \) is minimized subject to \( \sum \psi_i = 0 \) by the **Fiedler vector** \( \psi^{(2)} \), the eigenvector of \( L \) corresponding to the second-smallest eigenvalue \( \lambda_2 \).

The eigenvalues of \( L \) are
\[
0 = \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \leq \lambda_3 \leq \cdots \leq \lambda_N.
\]

The Fiedler vector satisfies
\[
L \psi^{(2)} = \lambda_2 \psi^{(2)}, \qquad \sum_i \psi^{(2)}_i = 0.
\]

The ground state energy is
\[
E_{\min} = \lambda_2 - \mu.
\]

For \( \mu < \lambda_2 \), the minimum is at \( \Psi = 0 \) (one subject).  
For \( \mu > \lambda_2 \), the minimum is at \( \Psi = c \, \psi^{(2)} \) for any \( c \neq 0 \) (two subjects).

---

## 3. The Partition

Define
\[
A_+ = \{ i \in V : \psi^{(2)}_i > 0 \}, \qquad A_- = \{ i \in V : \psi^{(2)}_i < 0 \}.
\]

These are the two alters. The boundary is
\[
\partial A_+ = \{ i \in A_+ : \exists j \in A_-, (i,j) \in E \},
\]
and similarly for \( \partial A_- \).

**Claim:** For \( \mu > \lambda_2 \) and \( \beta \) sufficiently large, the equilibrium measure concentrates on configurations proportional to \( \psi^{(2)} \), and the partition \( \{A_+, A_-\} \) satisfies S1–S4.

---

## 4. Verification of Subject Conditions

**S1 (Integration).**  
The subgraph induced by \( A_+ \) is connected if the Fiedler vector does not change sign within a connected component. For a connected graph, the Fiedler vector has both positive and negative entries, and the positive set is connected. The integration measure is
\[
\Phi(\Psi|_{A_+}) = \min_{\substack{B_1 \cup B_2 = A_+ \\ B_1 \cap B_2 = \emptyset}} I(\Psi|_{B_1} ; \Psi|_{B_2} \mid \Psi|_{\partial B_1 \cap \partial B_2}) > 0.
\]
This holds because \( \psi^{(2)} \) is nonzero on \( A_+ \) and the Gaussian measure has full support.

**S2 (Dissociation).**  
The boundary \( \partial A_+ \) screens off \( A_+ \) from \( A_- \). In the Gaussian model, the precision matrix is \( \Lambda = \beta (L - \mu I) \). The conditional independence \( A_+ \perp A_- \mid \partial A_+ \) holds if and only if \( \Lambda_{A_+ A_-} = 0 \). This is not exactly true for finite \( \beta \), but the off-diagonal block decays exponentially with the distance from the boundary. In the limit \( \beta \to \infty \), the measure concentrates on the ground state, and the conditional independence becomes exact.

**S3 (Self-model).**  
The self-model is encoded in the subgraph \( A_{\text{self}} \subseteq A_+ \) that has the highest integration. In the spectral solution, this is the set of nodes with the largest absolute Fiedler vector entries.

**S4 (Perspective).**  
The perspective is the sign of the Fiedler vector. Each alter has a unique perspective: \( A_+ \) experiences \( \psi > 0 \), \( A_- \) experiences \( \psi < 0 \). The uniqueness follows from the simplicity of \( \lambda_2 \) (for generic graphs).

---

## 5. The Critical Point

The transition occurs at
\[
\mu_c = \lambda_2.
\]

For \( \mu < \lambda_2 \): one subject (\( \Psi = 0 \)).  
For \( \mu > \lambda_2 \): two subjects (\( \Psi \propto \psi^{(2)} \)).

The critical inverse temperature is
\[
\beta_c = \frac{1}{\lambda_3 - \lambda_2},
\]
where \( \lambda_3 \) is the third-smallest eigenvalue. For \( \beta > \beta_c \), the equilibrium measure concentrates on the Fiedler vector.

---

## 6. Extension to \( K \) Alters

For \( K > 2 \), use the first \( K \) eigenvectors \( \psi^{(2)}, \dots, \psi^{(K)} \). The partition is determined by the sign pattern of the projection onto these eigenvectors. This is **spectral clustering**.

The energy becomes
\[
E[\Psi] = \sum_{k=2}^K \left( \Psi_k^T L \Psi_k - \mu \|\Psi_k\|^2 \right),
\]
where \( \Psi_k \) is the component of \( \Psi \) along \( \psi^{(k)} \).

The transition for \( K \) alters occurs at \( \mu_c = \lambda_K \).

---

## 7. Empirical Predictions

| Phenomenon | Prediction |
|:---|:---|
| **Psychedelics** | Reduce \( \mu \). At \( \mu < \lambda_2 \), alters merge into one subject. Neural signature: increased global integration, decreased modularity. |
| **Split-brain** | The corpus callosum is removed. The graph splits into two components. Each component has its own \( \lambda_2 \), yielding two independent alters. |
| **Anesthesia** | Increases \( \mu \) beyond \( \lambda_N \). All alters dissolve; \( \Psi = 0 \). |

- **Energy:** \( E[\Psi] = \Psi^T L \Psi - \mu \|\Psi\|^2 \), with \( \sum \psi_i = 0 \).
- **Ground state:** Fiedler vector \( \psi^{(2)} \).

- **Subject conditions:** S1–S4 hold for \( \mu > \lambda_2 \) and \( \beta > \beta_c \).
- **Extension:** \( K \) alters via the first \( K \) eigenvectors.

This is a complete, rigorous, and feasible solution. The decombination law is the spectral decomposition of the graph Laplacian. The alters are the spectral clusters. The boundary is the sign change of the Fiedler vector. ## 1. Corrected Energy: Stabilized Ginzburg–Landau on a Graph

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a connected graph. The experiential field is
\[
\Psi: V \to \mathbb{R}^d, \qquad \Psi_i \in \mathbb{R}^d.
\]
For \(d=1\), the model is a scalar field; for \(d>1\), it can support multiple ordered phases.

Define the free energy
\[
F[\Psi] = \sum_{i\in V} V(\Psi_i) + \frac{J}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in E} \|\Psi_i-\Psi_j\|^2,
\]
with a multi-well potential
\[
V(x) = \frac{g}{4}\big(\|x\|^2 - a\big)^2, \qquad g,a>0.
\]
This is bounded below. The equilibrium measure is
\[
p_\beta[\Psi] \propto \exp(-\beta F[\Psi]).
\]

The explicit stochastic dynamics are overdamped Langevin:
\[
d\Psi_i = -\frac{\partial F}{\partial \Psi_i}\,dt + \sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\,dW_i,
\]
where \(W_i\) are independent Wiener processes. This gives a well-defined sampling process. Time here is extrinsic; the fundamental object remains the equilibrium measure.

---

## 2. Symmetry Breaking and Amplitude

Linearize around the homogeneous state \(\Psi=0\). The quadratic part is
\[
F \approx \frac{1}{2}\Psi^T\big(J L - 4a I\big)\Psi,
\]
where \(L\) is the graph Laplacian. The homogeneous state is unstable when
\[
J \lambda_2 > 4a,
\]
where \(\lambda_2\) is the second-smallest eigenvalue of \(L\). The critical coupling is
\[
J_c = \frac{4a}{\lambda_2}.
\]
The unstable mode is the Fiedler vector \(\psi^{(2)}\). Near criticality, the amplitude scales as
\[
\|\Psi\| \propto \sqrt{\frac{J\lambda_2 - 4a}{g}}.
\]
This is the correct bifurcation law. It replaces the unsupported \(\beta_c\) formula.

The nonlinear domains are not simply the sign of the Fiedler vector. They are determined by the full nonlinear dynamics. The Fiedler vector gives the unstable direction at the transition; the final pattern depends on graph geometry, nonlinearity, and noise.

---

## 3. Operational Definitions of S1–S4

The spectral model does not by itself establish S1–S4. These must be defined as measurable criteria and checked numerically or empirically.

**S1 (Integration).**  
For a candidate alter \(A\subseteq V\), estimate
\[
\Phi(A) = \min_{\substack{A_1\cup A_2=A\\ A_1\cap A_2=\emptyset}} I\big(\Psi_{A_1};\Psi_{A_2}\mid \Psi_{\partial A_1\cap\partial A_2}\big).
\]
Require \(\Phi(A)>\theta\) for a threshold \(\theta\). This is an information-theoretic measure, not an eigenvector property.

**S2 (Dissociation / Markov blanket).**  
Find a minimal boundary \(\partial A\subseteq A\) such that
\[
I\big(\Psi_A;\Psi_{V\setminus A}\mid \Psi_{\partial A}\big) < \epsilon.
\]
This can be estimated from samples using conditional mutual information estimators. The boundary is not assumed to be the sign change of an eigenvector; it is discovered as a screening set.

**S3 (Self-model).**  
There must exist a subset \(A_{\text{self}}\subseteq A\) and a predictive map
\[
g: \Psi_{A_{\text{self}}} \to \Psi_{\partial A}
\]
whose prediction error is below a threshold. This is not automatic in the Ginzburg–Landau model. It must either emerge from additional structure or be added explicitly.

**S4 (Perspective).**  
There must be a unique fixed point \(\psi^*\) of the self-model map \(g\) in the support of \(p_\beta\). This formalizes “one first-person perspective.” Again, this is not guaranteed by domain formation.

## 5. What Remains Open

The corrected model is a valid **boundary-formation mechanism**, not yet a complete **subject-formation theory**. It shows how a homogeneous experiential field can spontaneously break into domains separated by Markov blankets. That is the first half of decombination.

The second half—self-model and perspective—requires additional structure. A scalar field has no self-model. A vector field with \(d>1\) can support multiple ordered phases, but still no first-person perspective. To get S3–S4, one needs either:

- an auxiliary generative-model field coupled to the order parameter,
- a hierarchical graph with nested Markov blankets,
- or a dynamical system with a stable fixed point representing the self.

These are genuine open problems, not engineering details. The decombination problem is therefore not fully solved. But it is now a well-posed research program with a rigorous first stage and clearly identified missing pieces.
