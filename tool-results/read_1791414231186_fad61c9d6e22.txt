   801→the neutral faces were thus salient ( Davis and Whalen, 2001;
   802→LeDoux, 1998 ) for ANP-simulating controls, they did not arouse
   803→them or attract much preconscious attention, as happened for au-
   804→thentic EP. The current ﬁndings add to the psychobiological evidence
   805→(Hermans et al., 2006; Reinders et al., 2012 ) that DID is neither an ef-
   806→fect of suggestion and fantasy, nor of role-playing.
   807→The ﬁndings have strong implications for the clinical context in deal-
   808→ing with DID patients and suggest that therapists of DID patients must be
   809→emotionally and behaviorally engaged. Therapeutic neutrality will prob-
   810→ably scare them, particularly as EP, triggering and reinforcing condi-
   811→tioned emotional and defensive reactions. As EP, these patients will
   812→tend to perceive an emotionally neutral therapist as an emotionally
   813→unavailable caretaker. These effects may not be immediately visible
   814→when an ANP is dominant due to ANP's mental avoidance and
   815→under-engagement. However, ANP and EP can be activated in parallel
   816→(Van der Hart et al., 2006), so that the therapist's neutrality can nonethe-
   817→less affect the patient as one or more EPs. This interpretation is consis-
   818→tent with clinical observations (Van der Hart et al., 2006). For example,
   819→ANP may report that EP is negatively affected by the therapist's neutral-
   820→ity. It may also happen that ANP does not notice or report this emotion-
   821→ality in an EP, but that EP responds in the described emotional sense in a
   822→later stage, while expressing that she/he felt rejected, confused, or afraid
   823→when the therapist was emotionally un(der)engaged.
   824→This study has several limitations. Our sample size was relatively
   825→small, which was due to the dif ﬁculty in ﬁnding DID patients who
   826→are able to alternate between ANP and EP at request and to remain ac-
   827→tivated, particularly as EP, for a substantial period of time in an fMRI
   828→environment. Patients who can perform this feat are the ones who
   829→have been in treatment for at least several years. Because treatment
   830→of DID fosters integration between the different dissociative parts
   831→and integration of traumatic memories, studies such as ours are
   832→prone to underestimate naturally existing biopsychosocial differences
   833→between these subsystems of the personality. In order to check if the
   834→actors had understood and followed the instructions to simulate an
   835→ANP and EP, patients and controls completed as ANP and EP the
   836→STAI-S ( Laux et al., 1981 ) immediately after the fMRI measurement.
   837→Explorative data analysis revealed that, in contrast to the behavioral
   838→and neural data, no inverse simulation pattern could be observed
   839→(see Table 1 ). That is, as ANP, the actors tended to react like ANP in
   840→DID patients, and as EP like EP in these patients. However, no signi ﬁ-
   841→cant differences were observed between and within groups. There-
   842→fore, the STAI-S does not seem to be an appropriate measurement to
   843→examine adherence to simulation instructions. In future studies,
   844→other assessments such as self-report should be included. DID pa-
   845→tients have considerable comorbidity ( Ellason et al., 1996 ). Future
   846→studies will need to evaluate axis I and axis II comorbidity and ad-
   847→dress covariations between this comorbidity and patterns of neural
   848→activation. Another limitation of the study is that only two of our pa-
   849→tients were free of medication. Medication washout is not feasible
   850→with DID patients. However, medication does not explain the ob-
   851→served differences between ANP and EP in DID patients.
   852→In conclusion, the current study shows that two prototypical parts
   853→of the personality in DID patients, ANP and EP, have different
   854→biopsychosocial reaction patterns to backward masked neutral and
   855→angry faces that controls were unable to simulate. Fixed in active de-
   856→fense, as EP, DID patients engage in early and automatic scanning of fa-
   857→cial expressions. Avoiding threat cues, as ANP, they are underinvolved
   858→in the faces. These results and interpretations are consistent with clini-
   859→cal observations and TSDP, but inconsistent with the sociocognitive
   860→model of DID.
