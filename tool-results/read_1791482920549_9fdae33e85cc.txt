    13→ ABSTRACT
    14→ BACKGROUND: While the exploration of serotonergic psychedelics as psychiatric 
    15→medicines deepens, so does the
    16→ pressure to better understand how these compounds act on the brain.
    17→ METHODS: We used a double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover design and 
    18→administered lysergic acid
    19→ diethylamide (LSD), 3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA), and d-amphetamine in 
    20→25 healthy participants.
    21→ By using spectral dynamic causal modeling, we mapped substance-induced changes in 
    22→effective connectivity
    23→ between the thalamus and different cortex types (unimodal vs. transmodal) derived 
    24→from a previous study with
    25→ resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging data. Due to the distinct 
    26→pharmacological modes of action of
    27→ the 3 substances, we were able to investigate speciﬁc effects mainly driven by 
    28→different neurotransmitter systems
    29→ on thalamocortical and corticothalamic interactions.
    30→ RESULTS: Compared with placebo, all 3 substances increased the effective 
    31→connectivity from the thalamus to
    32→ speciﬁc unimodal cortices, whereas the inﬂuence of these cortices on the 
    33→thalamus was reduced. These results
    34→ indicate increased bottom-up and decreased top-down information ﬂow between the 
    35→thalamus and some unimodal
    36→ cortices. However, for the amphetamines, we found the opposite effects when 
    37→examining the effective connectivity
    38→ with transmodal cortices, including parts of the salience network. Intriguingly, 
    39→LSD increased the effective
    40→ connectivity from the thalamus to both unimodal and transmodal cortices, 
    41→indicating a breach in the hierarchical
    42→ organization of ongoing brain activity.
    43→ CONCLUSIONS: The results advance our knowledge about the action of psychedelics on 
    44→the brain and reﬁne current
    45→ models aiming to explain the underlying neurobiological processes.
